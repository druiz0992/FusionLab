# 5. Camera calibration, feature tracking and visual odometry

Updated 2026-10-21. This describes the camera-only pipeline in processing order:
calibration, feature tracking, then the monocular VO estimator and its
diagnostics. Commands and options are in the [README](../README.md#camera-calibration);
measured outcomes are in [3. Experiment results](3_experiment_results.md).

## Camera calibration

Calibration is a separate stage after acquisition. It estimates a pinhole intrinsic
matrix and five distortion coefficients in the original stored RGB pixel
coordinates. It does not calibrate depth/IMU extrinsics or sensor timing.
API reference: [OpenCV calibration](https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/d9/d0c/group__calib3d.html).

### Print and measure

Use [checkerboard.pdf](calibration_target/checkerboard.pdf), generated by
`fusionlab calibration-target`. A4 landscape, 10 columns × 7 rows of squares,
25 mm nominal square size: 9 × 6 internal corners, the detector's default.
Print at Actual size / 100% with fit-to-page disabled, and keep the white border.
Mount flat on rigid backing; avoid wrinkles, gloss and reflections. Measure
several squares horizontally and vertically (4 squares should total 100 mm) and
pass the measured single-square size as `--square-mm`. Reprint if X/Y scales
differ or the board is warped.

### Capture

Record over USB with live preview (`sensor-stream listen --transport usb --live
--record --output-dir data/sessions/rgb_calibration`). Enable the same rear RGB
camera configuration used for later work; depth/IMU/GPS can be off. Keep
lens/zoom/resolution unchanged. Capture 20–30 distinct poses:

- Entire board visible with white border, sharp squares and no fingers covering corners.
- Centre, left/right, top/bottom, and near each image corner.
- Different distances, the board occupying roughly 25–70% of image width where
  practical; never clip the board.
- Tilt about both axes (~20–40°), plus some front-facing views.
- Pause about 1–2 seconds per pose; move slowly between poses to limit blur.

The stored frame may be sideways relative to the preview; do not rotate or resize
files. Detection/calibration uses the original frame dimensions. Autofocus,
stabilization or automatic lens changes can affect intrinsics; if the app offers
no controls, keep the same mode and treat calibration as configuration-dependent.
Keep the printed target for verification captures.

### Fit and review

`fusionlab calibrate-camera PATH --square-mm SIZE` samples every 15th RGB frame,
up to 40 accepted layouts (`--stride`, `--max-views`). Near-duplicate layouts are
rejected. Multiple streams require `--camera-stream ID`; mixed image dimensions
are rejected.

At least 15 distinct complete detections are required. `calibration/detections.json`
and annotated corner images are saved even if there are too few views to fit.
`calibration.json` contains K (pixels), distortion in k1,k2,p1,p2,k3 order, image
size, measured square size, selected paths, per-view pixel RMS, coverage/tilt
warnings and the OpenCV version. Every fifth accepted view is held out of intrinsic
fitting; its pose is solved separately for reprojection evaluation.

Status is always `needs_review`: examine corner images, coverage/pose warnings,
implausible parameters and held-out errors. Low error on a weak dataset does not
prove calibration accuracy. No automatic frame rejection or parameter application
is hidden. Repeated runs overwrite outputs; source images remain unchanged.
Printed geometry, producer processing, metric depth scale and precise registration
remain separate sources of uncertainty.

### Validate an existing fit without refitting

Collect a **separate** verification capture with the same camera configuration,
preferably with a measured printed target mounted flat, and evaluate the existing
intrinsics with `fusionlab validate-calibration` (`fusionlab.vision.calibration_validation`)
before fitting a replacement. The command checks image integrity and original dimensions, detects the board
described by the calibration and holds K/distortion fixed. Board pose is fitted
on four fifths of the corners and the remaining corners are scored. It writes
`summary.json` into a new directory and never edits images or the calibration.

This checks consistency, not independently measured board poses or metric
accuracy. Inspect coverage and target quality as well as pixel errors; a related
screen-target capture can share systematic errors. The selected phone fit passed a
preliminary check on two earlier captures (median view held-out-corner RMS
0.132/0.240 px), but a fresh printed-target validation remains pending. See the
[review and loss-window images](../data/sessions/walking/20261017T183215.982977Z-45b7f9c2/analysis/comparisons/calibration_review/README.md).

## Feature tracking

Feature tracking follows recognizable image points through consecutive frames.
A feature is a small patch with distinctive intensity changes, usually a corner
or textured detail. A plain wall gives few reliable features. A track ID means
the tracker believes it is following the same image patch; it is not a
recognized object or a permanent landmark ID.

### Algorithm

1. Decode recorded camera frames and resize them to at most 640 pixels wide.
   Verify each asset's size and SHA-256 hash before reading it.
2. Detect Shi–Tomasi corners, initially up to 400, spaced at least 10 pixels apart.
3. Use pyramidal Lucas–Kanade optical flow to find each patch in the next frame.
   Image pyramids allow tracking motion at several image resolutions.
4. Track each candidate back to the previous frame. Reject failed, out-of-image,
   nonfinite or inconsistent matches: the round-trip error must be at most one
   pixel at tracking resolution.
5. When at least eight matches remain, use fundamental-matrix RANSAC to reject
   geometrically inconsistent matches (threshold 1.5 tracking pixels). If
   estimation fails, retain the optical-flow survivors and record that the
   geometric check failed. This heuristic does not prove every surviving match
   is correct, especially with planar scenes, pure rotation or lens distortion.
6. Retain IDs for surviving tracks and detect new corners away from them to
   replenish the population. Lost IDs are never reused within a run.

Timestamp gaps over 0.25 s, repeated/regressing timestamps, camera-stream changes
or image-size changes reset the tracker. `--camera-stream` selects the stream.

### Reading the overlays

- Green dots: features that survived tracking from the previous frame.
- Orange dots: newly detected features, which have no motion correspondence yet.
- Green lines: accepted image motion between the previous and current frame.
- Text: frame index, surviving track count and newly detected feature count.

Overlays rotate 90° clockwise by default for the current phone captures
(`--display-rotation 0|90|180|270`). Rotation is applied only after tracking and
drawing; the text is drawn upright afterwards. Raw captures and saved tracking
coordinates are unchanged.

### Saved results and coordinates

`tracks.jsonl` records IDs, XY pixel coordinates, ages in frames, rejection counts,
geometry status, source/receive timestamps, reset reasons and segment IDs.
Coordinates refer to the resized, **unrotated** image, with +X right and +Y down.
`pixel_scale_xy` maps original pixels to tracking pixels componentwise. Each row
also records the display rotation and overlay path, so an overlay click needs its
display rotation undone before comparing it with saved coordinates.
`summary.json` records settings and aggregate counts; `overlays/` holds JPEGs.
In code, rows are read and written as typed `TrackFrame` records
(`fusionlab.vision.tracks`); use `read_track_frames` rather than parsing JSON directly.

The output is a set of image correspondences, not a camera trajectory. Objects,
lighting changes, blur and occlusion all affect tracks; high track counts alone
do not prove accurate localization.

## Monocular visual odometry pipeline

1. Shi–Tomasi corners and pyramidal LK tracking (above). Persistent IDs are image
   tracks, not globally recognized landmarks.
2. Load explicitly selected RGB calibration. Scale intrinsics to tracking pixels
   and remove distortion; display rotation never changes estimator coordinates.
3. Bootstrap from an essential-matrix pose and triangulate landmarks. Initial
   camera axes define each segment; bootstrap translation has unit arbitrary length.
4. Estimate subsequent world-to-camera poses with PnP/RANSAC against the map.
   Default acceptance requires 20 inliers and 50% support at 3 tracking pixels.
5. Optional pose-acceptance, keyframe, landmark-validation/refinement and bounded
   local-BA variants can be compared independently. Defaults remain baseline.
6. On tracking loss, reinitialize a separate segment. There is no relocalization,
   scale joining, loop closure or full-route reconstruction.

World-to-camera transforms satisfy `X_camera = R X_world + t`; camera center is
`-R.T t`. Camera axes follow original pixels: +X right, +Y down, +Z forward.
The viewer shows an initial-camera XZ projection, not a gravity-aligned floor plan.
Each segment has its own unknown scale. Pixel residuals use the mean tracking
focal length to express normalized-coordinate errors approximately in pixels.

`MonocularOdometry.process` returns a `FrameResult`
(`fusionlab.vision.odometry.frame_result`): status, segment, timing and pose are
typed fields; PnP, triangulation, keyframe and BA details are in its ordered
`diagnostics` dict. Each `poses.jsonl` row is its flat `to_json()` form.

### Experimental policy variants

The variants in step 5 live in `fusionlab.vision.odometry.policies`. Each
registry maps a CLI name to a policy object, and the estimator only asks it
questions, so `--help` choices come straight from the registries:

| Registry | Variants | What the policy decides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `POSE_ACCEPTANCE` | `baseline`, `refined` | Refine the PnP pose after the support gates (baseline) or before recounting support (refined) |
| `KEYFRAME_POLICIES` | `baseline`, `parallax` | When to triangulate against the reference keyframe (`decide()`) |
| `LANDMARK_POLICIES` | `baseline`, `validation`, `refinement`, `multiview` | Two switches: third-view validation of new landmarks, and multiview position refinement (`multiview` = both) |

To add a variant, register a new `PoseAcceptance`/`LandmarkPolicy` entry, or a
new class with `name`, `explicit` and `decide()` for a keyframe rule. Compare it
with baseline on common inputs and record the outcome in
[experiment results](3_experiment_results.md); do not make it a default without
that evidence.

`two-view` (uncalibrated pair geometry) and `camera-motion` (calibrated pair
rotation and translation direction) are standalone two-frame diagnostics that
share the same tracks.

### Optional local bundle adjustment

The implementation jointly refines up to 80 landmarks and variable poses in a
five-frame window, attempted every third processed frame. Two complete anchor
poses remain fixed to remove local frame/scale freedom; their errors are retained.
Sparse least squares uses soft-L1 loss, a 25-evaluation budget, positive-depth
checks and a current full-map support guard. A cost-decreasing bounded step can
be accepted before convergence; the report distinguishes this.

Historical online output poses are not rewritten when old window poses change.
Window corrections are logged, and internal references/observations are
synchronized. This is not a full smoother with marginalization. Most
selected-window updates still fail the current-map guard.

### Diagnostics versus estimator inputs

Constant-velocity prediction, spatial coverage and gyro comparison (`diagnose-vo`)
and map-maintenance statistics (`diagnose-map`) are offline diagnostics. They do not constrain VO or change its acceptance rules.
Predictions use source-time intervals and reset across discontinuities. All pose
hypotheses are evaluated on the same pre-update map. Accepted post-BA poses may fit
a changed map, so those diagnostic counts need not equal original acceptance counts.

Gyro rotation magnitudes can be compared without extrinsics, conditional on
source-clock alignment (`--imu-offset-ms` shifts it; zero offset is an assumption).
Full-axis comparison requires an explicitly supplied camera-from-IMU rotation
(`--camera-from-imu`); no identity alignment is silently assumed. Camera–IMU
timing/extrinsics have not been calibrated.
